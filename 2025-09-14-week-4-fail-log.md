# Week 4 Fail Log - Second Brain/Notetaking
---
This week we had to choose a notetaking format/program to test out. As it's now been a week since I started testing the one I chose-- [TiddlyWiki](https://tiddlywiki.com/)-- out, I'm ready to reflect on the process.

## First Annotation
My [first annotation](https://app.perusall.com/courses/fall-2025-anthropology-of-science-and-technology-anth-410r-950/the-proto-language-of-anthropological-practice-an-exhibition-of-original-fieldnotes-week-4?annotationId=Nma3kP8v2rmsWNcwA) was for "The 'proto-language' of anthropological practice". It's a musing of mine I couldn't shake when I started to read: just how much has been lost to unorganized or large quantities of notes? The ones people lose or the amount that no one wants to read through to find where someone's connected something together. Is it at all a similar amount that's been lost to things like article paywalls or data rot?

## Second Annotation
My [second annotation](https://app.perusall.com/courses/fall-2025-anthropology-of-science-and-technology-anth-410r-950/the-proto-language-of-anthropological-practice-an-exhibition-of-original-fieldnotes-week-4?annotationId=oyxwZrj35m3bbkyXK) was also for "The 'proto-language' of anthropological practice", and is a connection I've made to my observations in life of handwritten vs typed notes. From what I've seen, handwritten notes seem to be me spontaneous, shorter, and informal; whereas typed notes tend to be the opposite. I wonder what kind of preferences people have between the one and the other and if they view it the same way as I do?

## Third Annotation
My [third annotation](https://app.perusall.com/courses/fall-2025-anthropology-of-science-and-technology-anth-410r-950/ethnographic-fieldnotes-on-drawn-notes-and-their-potentials-week-4?annotationId=23PCkzgLi32k7ytCd) is for the reading "ethnoGRAPHIC field notes". I relate to drawing doodles or figures in my handwritten notes, and find it a far easier way of remembering things than in text. However, I also know how most of us are raised is NOT to doodle while doing something important, as we've often been chided about it in classes when younger.
