## First Annotation
My [first annotation](https://app.perusall.com/courses/fall-2025-anthropology-of-science-and-technology-anth-410r-950/an-artificial-environment-is-often-a-noisy-environment-week-8?annotationId=TDRR37vtwow8hcB9z) was for our reading, "An Artificial Environment is Often a Noisy Environment" by Kazuo Ueda, et. al. I ponder how parsing spoken languages that are not a person's native tongue may be affected by things like Audio Processing Disorder and if-- with or without APD-- something like noise-cancelling headphones would help?

## Second Annotation
My [second annotation](https://app.perusall.com/courses/fall-2025-anthropology-of-science-and-technology-anth-410r-950/seeing-hearing-feeling-week-8?annotationId=MwKLLnACewgG34FGs) was for our reading, "Seeing, Hearing, Feeling" by Paul Henley. I point out that audio _is_ important and contains a lot of meaning within it, which makes the lack of proper captioning for those who are hard-of-hearing or Deaf deeply frustrating because they lose out on meaning that hearing people recieve. This includes improper captioning where things might just state a language being spoken and not its translation or even a transcript of the written language being spoken, or when curse words are censored which infantalizes HoH & Deaf adults.
